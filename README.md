# Bank Customer Churn Analysis

## Project Overview
This project explores a bank customer dataset (`bank_churn_data.csv`) to identify key demographic factors that drive customer attrition (churn)[cite: 2]. By analyzing the distribution of customer age, education levels, and income categories, this repository provides data-driven interpretations and actionable retention strategies to minimize churn across various customer segments[cite: 2].

## Key Findings and Interpretations

### 1. Age Distribution and Churn Correlation
* **Customer Base Profile:** The majority of the bank's customers fall within the 35 to 55 age range, with both the average and median age situated at approximately 46 years old[cite: 2]. This indicates that the core demographic consists of working-age individuals approaching their pre-retirement phase[cite: 2].
* **High-Risk Age Groups:** The highest churn rate is observed in the 50–59 age bracket, reaching approximately 17.8%[cite: 2]. This is closely followed by the 60–69 age group (16.2%) and the 40–49 age group (14.5%)[cite: 2]. Conversely, the youngest demographic (20–29 years old) exhibits the lowest churn rate at just 8.5%[cite: 2].
* **Critical Churn Peaks:** Specific ages demonstrate alarmingly high attrition, particularly customers at age 62 (experiencing a churn rate of over 25%), as well as ages 65 and 53[cite: 2]. This strongly suggests that customers nearing or entering retirement are highly vulnerable to churning[cite: 2].

### 2. Education, Income, and Churn Dynamics
* **Socioeconomic Correlation:** There is a clear positive correlation between a customer's education level and their income[cite: 2]. Customers with higher educational backgrounds (Post-Graduate, Doctorate) predominantly fall into higher income brackets ($80K - $120K or $120K+)[cite: 2]. Meanwhile, "Uneducated" customers or those with only a "High School" diploma are heavily concentrated in the lowest income category (Less than $40K)[cite: 2].
* **Churn by Education Level:** "Uneducated" customers record the highest churn rate at 18.5%, followed by "High School" graduates at 16.2%[cite: 2]. Customers with Doctorate and Post-Graduate degrees are the most loyal, with the lowest churn rates hovering between 9% and 10%[cite: 2].
* **Churn by Income Level:** The "Less than $40K" income bracket experiences the highest attrition at 19.1%, whereas the highest income bracket ($120K+) has the lowest at 8.7%[cite: 2]. The data indicates that higher income directly correlates with a significantly lower likelihood of churning[cite: 2].
* **The Compounding Effect:** The intersection of low education and low income creates the highest risk segment, with churn rates exceeding 25%[cite: 2]. In contrast, the combination of high education and high income yields a highly stable customer segment with churn rates below 10%[cite: 2].

## Strategic Recommendations
Based on the exploratory data analysis, the following targeted retention strategies are recommended to improve customer loyalty:
* **Pre-Retirement & Senior Programs:** Develop tailored retention strategies specifically for customers aged 50 and above [cite: 2]. This can include exclusive loyalty programs, specialized pre-retirement financial products, and highly personalized banking services designed to secure their long-term trust [cite: 2].
* **Support for Vulnerable Socioeconomic Segments:** The low-income and low-education segment requires immediate prioritization for retention efforts [cite: 2]. The bank should consider offering financial literacy education, administrative fee subsidies, or light installment loan products to alleviate financial friction and reduce attrition in this demographic [cite: 2].
